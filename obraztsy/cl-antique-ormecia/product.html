The History of Ownership

Narrative of Provenance

Commissioned during the zenith of the Florentine Renaissance, this ceremonial ewer was crafted by an anonymous master silversmith within the inner circle of the Medici court. Its ornate engravings — executed with breathtaking precision — depict the allegorical triumphs of peace and civic virtue, a stark ideological contrast to the political turbulence of the era beneath the surface.

Records from the 1502 inventory of the Palazzo Medici describe "a vessel of exceptional silver bearing the lily of Florence upon its collar," almost certainly used during the nuptial feasts and state ceremonies of the ruling elite. It remained within the private chambers of the family until the dissolution of the senior branch's direct assets in the mid-18th century.

The ewer resurfaced in the documented collection of Sir Julian Goldsmid in 1891, catalogued as "Italian, XV century, of extraordinary quality," following its acquisition at a private sale of the Borghese estates. After passing through the custodianship of a private Swiss cultural trust, it reached its current state of exceptional preservation — a testament to the enduring legacy of Medici patronage and the technical brilliance of 15th-century Florentine metalwork.

Ormecia is proud to present this piece with one of the most complete ownership chains in our institutional history — spanning 559 years of documented custodianship.

Documented Ownership

559 Years of
Provenance

1465

Commission & Creation

Commissioned by Piero di Cosimo de' Medici for the private treasury of the Palazzo Medici, Florence. Crafted by an unnamed court silversmith, later identified through stylistic analysis as working within the workshop of Antonio del Pollaiolo.

1502

Nuptial Gift — Palazzo Medici Inventory

Listed in the household ledgers of Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco de' Medici as part of the formal inventory following the family's nuptial ceremonies. Described as "a vessel of silver, most beautifully wrought, with the lily of Florence."

1743

Roman Private Sale — Borghese Estate

Sold at an undocumented private disposal of Borghese family property in Rome. Acquired by an unnamed English Grand Tour collector, subsequently traced through the papers of the Goldsmid family archives in London.

1891

Sir Julian Goldsmid Collection, London

Formally catalogued in the estate inventory of Sir Julian Goldsmid, 1st Baronet, as "Florentine, XV century silver ewer, of extraordinary merit." Exhibited at the South Kensington Museum, London, 1892, catalogue reference 1892/471.

1989

Acquisition by Private Swiss Cultural Trust

Acquired by a private cultural foundation in Geneva, Switzerland, where it remained in climate-controlled storage and was lent to two international museum exhibitions over the subsequent three decades.

2024

Ormecia Fine Arts — Current Listing

Entrusted to Ormecia Fine Arts following a private consignment arrangement with the Swiss foundation. Full forensic authentication completed by our international panel. Now available for acquisition by a single discerning collector.

Scholarship & Conservation

Material & Technical
Analysis

Medium & Composition
Sterling Silver, 96.4% purity. XRF analysis confirms 15th-century Florentine alloy composition consistent with Medici-era metalwork. Minor traces of copper (3.4%) and gold gilding on the spout collar.
Dimensions & Weight
Height: 34 cm · Width (handle to spout): 24 cm · Diameter at base: 12 cm · Weight: 1.82 kg. Dimensions consistent with known inventory records from c. 1502.
Surface & Decorative Programme
Repoussé and chased relief decoration depicting the Triumph of Peace. Engraved foliate border on base and neck. The Medici palle device (six bezants) appears on the footring — a unique identifier matching described inventory references.
Condition Assessment
Museum Grade. Minor age-consistent patination. No significant losses or repairs. The handle, restored once c. 1880 with period-appropriate techniques, has been independently confirmed in conservation reports. Fully stable.
Conservation History
Light surface cleaning and stabilisation performed by the V&A Conservation Department, 2004. No structural interventions. Stored in inert-gas sealed archival condition since 1997.
Authentication Methods
X-Ray Fluorescence · Stylistic Audit · Archival Research (5 independent sources) · Metallurgical Cross-Section Analysis · Comparative Database Matching (Corpus Ornamentale Florentinae)
